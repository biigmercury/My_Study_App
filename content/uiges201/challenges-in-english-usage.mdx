This lesson covers the errors most often corrected in Nigerian university English, using standard British usage (the norm for Nigerian schools) as the reference.

## Why Errors Happen

English is a **second language (L2)** for most Nigerians, learned alongside one or more mother tongues (L1) and Nigerian Pidgin. Most errors have identifiable causes:

| Cause | What happens | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Mother-tongue interference (transfer)** | An L1 structure or idiom is carried into English | *"I heard the smell of the soup"* — Yoruba *gbọ́ òórùn*, literally "hear the smell" |
| **Over-generalisation** | A rule is applied where it doesn't belong | *"furnitures", "informations"* — adding plural -s to every noun |
| **Analogy** | A pattern from one word is copied to another | *"discuss about"* from *"talk about"* |
| **Pidgin influence** | Pidgin forms slip into formal English | *"on the light", "borrow me your pen"* |
| **Ignorance of exceptions** | The learner doesn't know a rule's limits | *"more better", "the news are bad"* |
| **Text-speak and haste** | Abbreviations and unchecked writing | *"pls, u, 2day, wat"* in an exam script |

**Nigerian English** is a legitimate variety with its own vocabulary (*go-slow, danfo, mama put*). The problem is not having a Nigerian accent or idiom. The problem is using forms that **block understanding** or that **formal and international readers count as errors**. **Know the standard form, and choose it in formal writing.**

## 1. Concord (Subject–Verb Agreement)

The verb agrees with its **subject** in number — and the subject is not always the noun nearest the verb.

| Rule | Correct |
|---|---|
| **One of** + plural noun takes a **singular** verb (the subject is "one") | *One of the students **is** absent.* |
| **Each, every, everyone, nobody, either, neither** are singular | *Each of the boys **has** a ticket.* / *Everyone **was** present.* |
| **Neither … nor / either … or**: the verb agrees with the **nearer** subject | *Neither the students nor the lecturer **was** there.* / *Neither the lecturer nor the students **were** there.* |
| **As well as, together with, along with, including** don't change the subject | *The Vice-Chancellor, as well as the deans, **is** coming.* |
| **The number of** = singular; **a number of** = plural | *The number of applicants **has** risen.* / *A number of applicants **have** complained.* |
| Nouns that **look plural but are singular** | *The news **is** good.* / *Mathematics **is** my best subject.* / *Measles **is** contagious.* |
| **Amounts** treated as one sum | *Five thousand naira **is** too much for a handout.* |
| **Collective nouns**: singular as a unit; plural (in British usage) when thinking of the members | *The committee **has** reached a decision.* / *The committee **are** divided on the issue.* |
| **Two nouns forming one idea** | *Bread and butter **is** my usual breakfast.* |

<ErrorSpotter title="Spot the concord error">

```errors
One of the boys [are=>is] absent today. :: The subject is "one", which is singular.
Each of the candidates [have=>has] been given a number. :: "Each" is singular.
The news about the strike [are=>is] disturbing. :: "News" is uncountable and takes a singular verb.
The Dean, together with the HODs, [were=>was] at the meeting. :: "Together with" does not make the subject plural.
A number of students have complained about the hostel. :: "A number of" takes a plural verb.
Neither the lecturer nor the students [was=>were] aware of the change. :: The verb agrees with the nearer subject, "students".
The number of failures [are=>is] alarming. :: "The number" is singular.
Mathematics is compulsory for science students. :: The subject name is singular.
```

</ErrorSpotter>

## 2. Countable and Uncountable Nouns

**Uncountable (mass) nouns** take **no plural -s** and **no "a/an"**, and they take a **singular verb**. To count them, use a unit word: *a piece of, an item of, a set of*.

| Uncountable — never add -s | Say instead |
|---|---|
| ~~furnitures~~ → **furniture** | *three pieces of furniture* |
| ~~equipments~~ → **equipment** | *two items of equipment* |
| ~~informations~~ → **information** | *a piece of information* |
| ~~advices~~ → **advice** | *some advice, a word of advice* |
| ~~luggages / baggages~~ → **luggage / baggage** | *two bags* |
| ~~stationeries~~ → **stationery** | *some stationery* |
| ~~jewelleries~~ → **jewellery** | *a piece of jewellery* |
| ~~machineries~~ → **machinery** | *a machine, some machinery* |
| ~~evidences~~ → **evidence** | *a piece of evidence* |

**Staff** and **personnel** are collective. One person is a **staff member** or **member of staff**, not "a staff": *She is a member of staff*, not ~~*She is a staff*~~.

```sort
furniture => Uncountable (no -s) :: three pieces of furniture
chair => Countable (can take -s) :: three chairs

fact => Countable (can take -s) :: two facts

tool => Countable (can take -s) :: many tools
```

## 3. Redundancy (Tautology)

**Redundancy** is saying the same thing twice. It wastes words and suggests you don't know what the words mean (remember **economy**).

| Redundant | Better | Why |
|---|---|---|
| return **back**, reply **back**, revert **back** | return, reply, revert | *re-* already means "back" |
| repeat **again** | repeat | "repeat" means "do again" |
| join **together**, cooperate **together** | join, cooperate | joining is "together" |
| **free** gift | gift | a gift is free |
| **past** history | history | history is past |
| **final** conclusion | conclusion | a conclusion is final |
| ATM **machine**, PIN **number** | ATM, PIN | the M is "machine"; the N is "number" |
| **new** innovation | innovation | an innovation is new |
| the reason is **because** | the reason is **that** | "reason" already means "because" |
| **each and every** | each / every | one is enough |
| at **about** 2 p.m. | at 2 p.m. / about 2 p.m. | choose exact or approximate |

## 4. Prepositions

Some verbs take **no preposition**, even though the related **noun** does. Others need a particular one.

| Wrong | Right | Note |
|---|---|---|
| discuss **about** the issue | **discuss** the issue | but: a *discussion about* |
| emphasise **on** punctuality | **emphasise** punctuality | but: an *emphasis on* |
| request **for** a transfer | **request** a transfer | but: a *request for* |
| comprise **of** five chapters | **comprise** / **consist of** five chapters | |
| investigate **into** the matter | **investigate** the matter | but: an *investigation into* |
| order **for** a book / demand **for** payment | **order** a book / **demand** payment | the nouns take "for" |
| voice **out** your concerns | **voice** your concerns / **speak out** | |
| cope **up** with stress | **cope with** stress | |
| congratulate him **for** his success | congratulate him **on** his success | |
| married **with** a doctor | married **to** a doctor | |
| **on** the long run / **in** the other hand | **in** the long run / **on** the other hand | |
| regret **for** coming late | **regret** coming late | |

## 5. Confusable Words

| Pair | Meaning and example |
|---|---|
| **advice** (noun) / **advise** (verb) | *Take my **advice**; I **advise** you to read.* |
| **affect** (verb) / **effect** (noun) | *The strike **affected** us; its **effect** was severe.* |
| **lose** / **loose** | *Don't **lose** your ID card; the screw is **loose**.* |
| **principal** / **principle** | *The **principal** (head; main) explained the **principle** (rule).* |
| **stationary** / **stationery** | ***Stationary** = not moving; **stationery** = writing materials ("e" for envelope).* |
| **compliment** / **complement** | *She **complimented** (praised) my essay; the sauce **complements** (completes) the rice.* |
| **accept** / **except** | *I **accept** all the conditions **except** the last.* |
| **borrow** / **lend** | *Can I **borrow** your pen? = Can you **lend** me your pen?* |
| **later** / **latter** | ***Later** = afterwards; **latter** = the second of two.* |
| **quite** / **quiet** | *The hall was **quite** (fairly) **quiet** (silent).* |
| **council** / **counsel** | *The Students' **Council** sought legal **counsel** (advice; a lawyer).* |
| **eminent** / **imminent** | *An **eminent** (distinguished) professor; an **imminent** (about to happen) strike.* |
| **practice** (noun) / **practise** (verb) — British | *Teaching **practice**; **practise** your speech.* |
| **personal** / **personnel** | ***Personal** = private; **personnel** = staff.* |
| **flout** / **flaunt** | *He **flouted** (disobeyed) the rules and **flaunted** (showed off) his car.* |

<ErrorSpotter title="Spot the wrong word">

```errors
Please, can you [borrow=>lend] me your calculator? :: You borrow FROM someone; you lend TO someone.
The lecturer gave us useful [advise=>advice] on the project. :: The noun is "advice"; "advise" is the verb.
The strike will [effect=>affect] our examination timetable. :: "Affect" is the verb; "effect" is usually a noun.
Make sure you don't [loose=>lose] your admission letter. :: "Lose" = misplace; "loose" = not tight.
We bought pens and envelopes from the [stationary=>stationery] shop. :: Stationery = writing materials.
The principal explained the principle behind the new rule. :: "Principal" = head of school; "principle" = rule.
He [flaunted=>flouted] the hall regulations and was suspended. :: Flout = disobey; flaunt = show off.
```

</ErrorSpotter>

## 6. Tense and Verb Forms

| Wrong | Right | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| *I **have seen** him yesterday.* | *I **saw** him yesterday.* | No present perfect with a **finished past time** (yesterday, last week, in 2020) |
| *I have been here **since** three years.* | *…**for** three years* / *…since 2023* | **For** + a period; **since** + a starting point |
| *I **am having** a car.* / *I **am understanding** you.* | *I **have** a car.* / *I **understand** you.* | **Stative verbs** (have = own, know, understand, believe, like) are rarely used in the progressive |
| *He **did not went**.* / *Did she **came**?* | *He **did not go**.* / *Did she **come**?* | After **did**, use the base form |
| *I was **opportuned** to attend.* | *I **had the opportunity** to attend.* | **Opportune** is an adjective, not a verb |
| *We are **suppose to** submit today.* | *We are **supposed to**…* | The past participle is needed |
| *I **use to** read at night.* (past habit) | *I **used to** read at night.* | Past habit = **used to** + base verb |

## 7. Pronouns, Comparison and Question Tags

**Pronouns.**
- After a preposition, use the **object** form: *between you and **me*** (not ~~*between you and I*~~), *for him and **me***.
- As a subject, use the **subject** form, and **put yourself last**: ***Tunde and I** went* (not ~~*Myself and Tunde went*~~ or ~~*Me and Tunde*~~).
- **Myself** is reflexive or emphatic (*I hurt **myself***; *I did it **myself***). It is not a polite form of "I" or "me".
- Use **who** as subject, **whom** as object: *the man **who** (I think) did it*; *the man **whom** we met*.

**Comparison.**
- Never double the comparative: ~~*more better*~~ → **better**; ~~*most cleverest*~~ → **cleverest**.
- **Senior, junior, superior, inferior, prior, prefer** take **to**, not *than*:
  - ~~*superior than*~~ → **superior to**;
  - ~~*I prefer rice than beans*~~ → **I prefer rice to beans**.
- ~~*He is senior to me with two years*~~ → ***He is two years older than me*** / ***senior to me by two years***.

**Question tags.** The tag repeats the **auxiliary and subject** of the statement, with the opposite polarity. ***"Isn't it?"*** is not a universal tag:

| Statement | Wrong tag | Right tag |
|---|---|---|
| *You are coming,* | ~~isn't it?~~ | **aren't you?** |
| *He can swim,* | ~~isn't it?~~ | **can't he?** |
| *They didn't pay,* | ~~isn't it?~~ | **did they?** |
| *Let's go,* | ~~isn't it?~~ | **shall we?** |
| *I am right,* | ~~amn't I?~~ | **aren't I?** |

## 8. Nigerianisms: Know the Standard Equivalent

These are common and understood in Nigeria, and many are fine in casual Nigerian speech. In **formal and international** writing, use the standard form.

| Nigerian usage | Standard international English |
|---|---|
| *On / off the light.* | **Switch on / switch off** the light. |
| *Borrow me your pen.* | **Lend** me your pen. |
| *I'm coming* (said while leaving briefly) | **I'll be right back.** |
| *next tomorrow* | **the day after tomorrow** |
| *my senior brother* | my **elder / older** brother |
| *go-slow* (traffic jam) | **traffic jam**. In British English a *go-slow* is a deliberate **work slowdown** by employees. |
| *Flash me.* | **Give me a missed call.** |
| *I've told him **severally**.* | I've told him **several times**. Standard *severally* means **separately, individually**, as in *jointly and severally liable*. |
| *I heard the smell.* | I **smelt / noticed** the smell. |
| *He is my **tight** friend.* | He is my **close** friend. |
| *I'll **drop** at the junction.* (from a bus) | I'll **get off** at the junction. |

```sort

Please switch on the fan. => Standard form :: Correct.
```

## 9. Spelling

Spelling errors cost marks in **every** course. These words are misspelt again and again:

| Correct spelling | Common misspelling | Memory aid |
|---|---|---|
| **accommodation** | acommodation, accomodation | double **c**, double **m** — it accommodates both |
| **embarrass** | embarass | double **r**, double **s** |
| **separate** | seperate | there is **a rat** in sep**a**rate |
| **definitely** | definately | it contains "finite" |
| **receive** | recieve | **i** before **e** except after **c** |
| **occurrence** | occurence | double **c**, double **r**, **-ence** |
| **committee** | commitee, comittee | double **m**, double **t**, double **e** |
| **privilege** | priviledge | no **d** |
| **pronunciation** | pronounciation | pro**nun**ciation — the "noun" disappears |
| **maintenance** | maintainance | main**ten**ance |
| **government** | goverment | gover**n**ment — govern + ment |
| **necessary** | neccessary, necesary | one **c**ollar, two **s**leeves |
| **questionnaire** | questionaire | double **n** |
| **February** | Febuary | Feb-**ru**-ary |
| **millennium** | millenium | double **l**, double **n** |
| **conscience** / **conscious** | conscience used for "aware" | con**science** = moral sense; conscious = awake, aware |

**British or American?** Nigerian education follows **British spelling**: *colour, centre, programme* (a computer *program*), *travelled, defence, cheque, licence* (noun). Both *-ise* and *-ize* are acceptable in British English. Whichever you use, **be consistent** throughout a piece of writing — set your word processor's language to *English (UK)*.

## Mixed Practice

<ErrorSpotter title="Mixed errors">

```errors
The students were asked to [discuss about=>discuss] the poem in groups. :: "Discuss" takes a direct object — no "about".
I [have seen=>saw] the Dean yesterday. :: A finished past time (yesterday) takes the simple past.
We bought new [furnitures=>furniture] for the common room. :: "Furniture" is uncountable.
Between you and [I=>me], the test was difficult. :: After a preposition, use the object form.
This year's result is [more better=>better] than last year's. :: Don't double the comparative.
Please [return back=>return] the book by Friday. :: "Return" already means "go back".
You are coming to the lecture, [isn't it=>aren't you]? :: The tag repeats "are" + "you".
I prefer reading in the library [than=>to] in my room. :: Prefer … to.
The committee has submitted its report. :: The committee is treated as one unit, so "has" and "its" agree.
She is a [staff=>member of staff] of the Faculty of Arts. :: "Staff" is collective; one person is a member of staff.
The Vice-Chancellor [emphasised on=>emphasised] the need for discipline. :: The verb takes no "on"; only the noun does ("emphasis on").
I was [opportuned=>privileged] to meet the Governor. :: "Opportune" is an adjective; say "I had the opportunity" or "I was privileged".
```

</ErrorSpotter>

## Useful Interventions: a Personal Improvement Plan

<FlowDiagram title="How to keep improving your English">
<FlowStep title="1. Read widely and daily">
**Extensive reading** (see *Reading Comprehension and Register*) is the most powerful cure: correct forms become familiar, and wrong ones start to "sound wrong".
</FlowStep>
<FlowStep title="2. Use a learner's dictionary">
For example the *Oxford Advanced Learner's Dictionary* or the *Longman Dictionary of Contemporary English*. They show whether a noun is **countable [C] or uncountable [U]**, which **prepositions** a word takes, its **pronunciation** and example sentences.
</FlowStep>
<FlowStep title="3. Keep an error log">
Write down every correction from marked scripts: the wrong form, the right form, and the rule. Review it before each test.
</FlowStep>
<FlowStep title="4. Proofread deliberately">
Read your work **aloud**. Read it **backwards** to catch spelling mistakes. Check concord, tense and punctuation in separate passes.
</FlowStep>
<FlowStep title="5. Use tools critically">
Spell-checkers and grammar checkers catch many slips but **miss real-word errors** (*loose/lose*, *their/there*) and sometimes suggest wrong "corrections". You make the final decision.
</FlowStep>
<FlowStep title="6. Consult usage guides and good models">
For example Michael Swan's *Practical English Usage*. Listen to standard models (news broadcasts, lectures) for pronunciation and idiom.
</FlowStep>
<FlowStep title="7. Code-switch consciously">
Pidgin and Nigerianisms belong to their settings. **Formal writing and examinations need the standard form.**
</FlowStep>
</FlowDiagram>

<Takeaway>
- **Causes of errors:** mother-tongue transfer, over-generalisation, analogy, Pidgin, ignorance of exceptions, text-speak.
- **Concord:** *one of … is*; *each/everyone* singular; *neither … nor* agrees with the nearer subject; *as well as* doesn't add; *the number* singular, *a number* plural; *news, mathematics* singular.
- **Uncountables** never take -s: furniture, equipment, information, advice, luggage, stationery, evidence. One person is a **member of staff**.
- **Redundancy:** return back, repeat again, free gift, PIN number, "the reason is because".
- **Prepositions:** discuss, emphasise, request, investigate (no preposition); comprise (no "of"); congratulate **on**; married **to**; prefer/superior **to**.
- **Confusables:** advice/advise, affect/effect, lose/loose, stationary/stationery, borrow/lend, flout/flaunt.
- **Verbs:** no present perfect with *yesterday*; *for* a period, *since* a point; no progressive with stative verbs; *did* + base form.
- **Pronouns and tags:** *between you and me*; *Tunde and I*; tags repeat the auxiliary (*aren't you? can't he?*).
- **Nigerianisms** have their place, but formal writing needs the standard form. **Spell** carefully and consistently (British).
- **Interventions:** read widely, use a learner's dictionary, keep an error log, proofread aloud, use checkers critically.
</Takeaway>

## Exam Tips

- In "**choose the correct option**" and "**spot the error**" questions, find the **true subject** first, then check the verb, the preposition and the word form.
- Look out for the **"reasonable-sounding" error**: forms you hear every day (*discuss about*, *borrow me*, *isn't it?*) are exactly the ones examiners test.
- Spend the last five minutes of every written exam **proofreading** for concord, tense and spelling. These are the cheapest marks to recover.
