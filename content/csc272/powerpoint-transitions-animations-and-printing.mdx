## Making the Presentation "Come Alive"

In the previous lesson you built the slides of **"My Journey in UI so far"**. Now you will **run** it as a slide show, add **special effects** — transitions, animations, progressive disclosure, footers — rehearse its timing, and **print** it.

---

## Running the Slide Show

| How you start | Where it starts |
|---|---|
| **F5**, or SLIDE SHOW → **From Beginning** | Slide 1 |
| **Shift+F5**, SLIDE SHOW → **From Current Slide**, or the **Slide Show** button on the status bar | The slide currently selected |

**Moving through the show:**

| Go forward | Go back | Other |
|---|---|---|
| Left mouse **click** | **←** (left arrow) | Type a **slide number + Enter** to jump to it |
| **Enter**, **N**, **→**, **↓**, **Spacebar**, **Page Down** | **P**, **↑**, **Backspace**, **Page Up** | **Home** = first slide, **End** = last slide |

**Right-click** during the show for the pop-up menu:

| Command | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Next / Previous** | One step forward or back |
| **Last Viewed** | Back to the slide you were on before jumping out of sequence |
| **See All Slides** | Thumbnails of every slide — click one to jump to it |
| **Zoom In** | Magnify part of the slide |
| **Screen → Black Screen / White Screen** | Blank the screen to pull attention back to you (keys **B** or **.** / **W** or **,** — press any key to return) |
| **Show Taskbar** | Displays the Windows taskbar to switch to another program |
| **Pointer Options → Laser Pointer / Pen / Highlighter** | Point at or draw on the slide (**Ctrl+L** laser, **Ctrl+P** pen, **Ctrl+A** back to the arrow) |
| **Ink Color, Eraser, Erase All Ink on Slide** | Change the pen colour or remove drawings (**E** erases all ink) |
| **End Show** | Stop the show (**Esc** or **-**) |

After the last slide PowerPoint shows a **black screen: "End of slide show, click to exit."** If you drew on the slides, it asks whether to **Keep** or **Discard** your ink annotations — keeping them changes the original slides.

## Slide Transitions

A **transition** controls **how a slide arrives** on the screen: instead of jumping abruptly, it can fade, push, wipe or split into view. Transitions make a show look polished — **don't get carried away**.

1. In **Normal** view select the slide (e.g. slide 1).
2. Open the **TRANSITIONS** tab. The **Transition to This Slide** group dominates the ribbon; click an effect to preview it (the **More** arrow shows them all: *Subtle*, *Exciting* and *Dynamic Content*).
3. A small **star symbol** appears beside the slide's thumbnail — the sign that it has a transition.
4. **Effect Options** changes the direction or variation (e.g. **From Right**, **From Top**).
5. **Duration** (Timing group) sets how long the effect takes — increase it to slow the effect down.
6. **Sound** adds a sound to the transition (rarely a good idea).
7. **Advance Slide**: **On Mouse Click** (the presenter controls it) and/or **After** a set time (the show moves on by itself).
8. **Apply To All** gives every slide the same transition and timing.

<ExamTip>
Different transitions on every slide may "add interest", but it is **not good practice** — it distracts from the talk and hurts accessibility. **Stick to one transition throughout**: set it on one slide, then click **Apply To All**. (To clear transitions, choose **None** and Apply To All.)
</ExamTip>

In PowerPoint **2007** transitions were on the *Animations* tab — which is why older handouts say "Animations tab → Transition to This Slide". From **2010** onward they have their own **TRANSITIONS** tab.

## Animations and Progressive Disclosure

**Transitions** act on the whole slide as it arrives. **Animations** act on **objects on a slide** — a title, a picture, each bullet point.

Animating a bulleted list so the points appear **one at a time** is called **progressive disclosure**. It stops the audience reading ahead of you and makes them concentrate on each point in turn.

1. Open the **ANIMATIONS** tab and click the bulleted placeholder on a slide.
2. Point at the effects in the **Animation** gallery to preview them; the **More** button shows all of them.
3. Choose one, e.g. **Fade**. Small numbers **1, 2, 3…** appear beside the bullets — the **click order**.
4. **Effect Options** sets the direction and the **Sequence**: **As One Object**, **All at Once**, or **By Paragraph** (one bullet per click). Older versions called this "by 1st level paragraphs".
5. The **Timing** group sets **Start** (**On Click**, **With Previous**, **After Previous**), **Duration** and **Delay**.

| Effect family | Colour in the gallery | Examples | Use |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Entrance** | Green | Appear, Fade, Fly In, Wipe, Zoom | How an object comes onto the slide |
| **Emphasis** | Yellow | Pulse, Grow/Shrink, Spin, Colour Pulse | Draw attention to something already there |
| **Exit** | Red | Disappear, Fade, Fly Out | How an object leaves the slide |
| **Motion Paths** | — | Lines, Arcs, Turns, Custom Path | Move an object along a route |

More tools: **Animation Pane** (lists every effect in order, lets you reorder and time them), **Add Animation** (a second effect on the same object), **Animation Painter** (copy an animation to another object, like Format Painter), **Trigger** (start an effect when a particular object is clicked).

As with transitions: **don't use too many different animations**, and avoid the more "exciting" ones — they distract and harm accessibility.

### Try it

1. On the **TRANSITIONS** tab choose a transition for slide 1, change the Effect Options and Duration, then click **Apply To All** — every thumbnail gets a ★.
2. On the **ANIMATIONS** tab select slide 2 and choose **Fly In** by paragraph — note the click numbers.
3. On the **SLIDE SHOW** tab click **From Beginning** and control the show with the keys: **N / → / Space** (next), **P / ←** (back), **B** and **W** (blank screen), a **number + Enter** (jump), **Esc** (end).
4. Try **Rehearse Timings**, then answer *Yes* to keep them — see the timings under the thumbnails in the Slide Sorter.

## Headers, Footers, Date and Slide Numbers

1. INSERT → Text group → **Header & Footer**.
2. Tick **Date and time**: **Update automatically** (always shows today's date, in a format you choose) or **Fixed** (the text you type).
3. Tick **Slide number**.
4. Tick **Footer** and type the text (e.g. your name and matric number).
5. Tick **Don't show on title slide** — you rarely want this information on slide 1.
6. **Apply to All** (every slide) or **Apply** (current slide only).

The **Notes and Handouts** tab of the same dialog adds a **header** and footer to printed notes pages and handouts (slides themselves have footers only).

## Rehearsing Timings

Rehearse before presenting to a real audience. PowerPoint can even record how long each slide (or each bullet) stays up, so the show can run **unattended** — for example on a screen at an **Open Day**.

1. SLIDE SHOW → Set Up group → **Rehearse Timings**.
2. The first slide appears with a **Recording** toolbar in the top-left corner. There are **two clocks**: the time for **this slide** and the **total** time.
3. Move to the next slide as usual. If you fumble, click **Repeat** (between the two clocks) to restart the clock for that slide. **Pause** stops the clock.
4. At the end PowerPoint says *"The total time for the slide show was … Do you want to keep the new slide timings?"* — click **Yes**.
5. Switch to **Slide Sorter**: each slide shows its timing underneath.
6. Press **F5**: the show now advances by itself. You can still click or press Enter to move on earlier.

To stop using the timings, **untick Use Timings** in the SLIDE SHOW → Set Up group.

### Other show settings

| Command (SLIDE SHOW tab) | Purpose |
|---|---|
| **Set Up Slide Show** | *Presented by a speaker (full screen)*, *Browsed by an individual (window)*, or *Browsed at a kiosk (full screen)* — kiosk mode loops continuously until **Esc**; also "Loop continuously until Esc", which slides to show, pen colour, advance manually or using timings |
| **Hide Slide** | Skip a slide in the show |
| **Record Slide Show** | Record **narration**, ink and laser-pointer movements with the timings — then FILE → Export → **Create a Video** to share it |
| **Use Presenter View** | Your notes, next slide and a timer on your screen; only the slide on the projector |
| **Custom Slide Show** | Present a chosen subset of slides in a chosen order |

## Slide Size (for Anything Other Than a Screen Show)

If the presentation will be used for anything other than an on-screen show — a **card**, a **poster**, printed pages — set the slide size **first**, before designing:

DESIGN → Customize group → **Slide Size** → **Standard (4:3)**, **Widescreen (16:9)** (the 2013 default) or **Custom Slide Size…**, where *Slides sized for* offers:

| Option | For |
|---|---|
| **On-screen Show** (4:3, 16:9, 16:10) | Projected presentations (the default) |
| **A3 / A4 Paper**, Letter, Ledger, B4, B5 | Printed pages |
| **35mm Slides** | Photographic slides |
| **Overhead** | Overhead-projector transparencies |
| **Banner** | Banner headings, e.g. on a display board |
| **Custom** | Any width and height — **conference posters**, **greeting cards** |

You can also change **Orientation** (portrait/landscape) for slides and for notes/handouts.

<ExamTip>
The practical question *"Design a 'Happy New Year' card in PowerPoint; your personal logo should be at the bottom of the back of the card"* tests exactly this: set a **custom slide size** in the card's shape, use a **Blank** layout, design the **front** on slide 1 and the **back** on slide 2 (logo placed at the bottom), apply colours with good contrast, and **save** with your matric number in the file name.
</ExamTip>

## Printing a Presentation

FILE → **Print** shows **Print Preview** on the right and the settings on the left.

1. Choose the **printer** and the number of **Copies**.
2. Under **Settings / Slides** choose *Print All Slides*, *Print Selection*, *Print Current Slide* or a **Custom Range** (e.g. `1-3,6`).
3. Choose **what** to print:

| Print layout | What you get | Who it is for |
|---|---|---|
| **Full Page Slides** | One slide per page | Overhead transparencies, a printed copy of each slide |
| **Notes Pages** | A small copy of the slide plus your speaker notes on each page | **You**, the presenter |
| **Outline** | Only the text of the slides | A quick reading copy of the content |
| **Handouts** | **1, 2, 3, 4, 6 or 9** slides per page (3 per page adds lines for writing notes) | The **audience** — saves paper |

4. Choose **Color**, **Grayscale** or **Pure Black and White**, collation and orientation, then **Print**.

Other outputs from FILE → **Export**: **Create PDF/XPS**, **Create a Video**, **Package Presentation for CD**, **Create Handouts** (sends slides and notes to Word).

## Closing

1. Save the latest version: **Ctrl+S**.
2. Close PowerPoint with the **Close (✕)** button.

**Alt+F4** closes any Office application; **Ctrl+F4** (or Ctrl+W) closes just the current file.

<Reveal question="1. Describe how to give every slide in a presentation the same transition that advances automatically after 5 seconds.">
Select a slide → TRANSITIONS tab → choose the transition (Effect Options and Duration as desired) → in the Timing group tick **After** and set **00:05.00** (optionally keep On Mouse Click too) → click **Apply To All**. Each thumbnail shows a star and the timing appears in Slide Sorter.
</Reveal>

<Reveal question="2. What is progressive disclosure and how is it achieved in PowerPoint?">
Revealing information **one item at a time** so the audience cannot read ahead and concentrates on each point. Select the bulleted placeholder → ANIMATIONS → an entrance effect (e.g. Fade) → Effect Options → Sequence **By Paragraph**, with Start **On Click**.
</Reveal>

<Reveal question="3. Explain the steps to rehearse timings and how to stop using them.">
SLIDE SHOW → **Rehearse Timings**. Present each slide while the Recording toolbar times it (Repeat restarts a slide's clock). At the end answer **Yes** to keep the new timings; they show under each slide in Slide Sorter and the show then advances by itself. To stop using them, untick **Use Timings** on the SLIDE SHOW tab.
</Reveal>

<Reveal question="4. How do you add slide numbers and a footer to every slide except the title slide?">
INSERT → **Header & Footer** → tick **Slide number** and **Footer** (type the footer text), optionally **Date and time** → tick **Don't show on title slide** → **Apply to All**.
</Reveal>

<Reveal question="5. List the four print layouts available in PowerPoint and who each is for.">
**Full Page Slides** (one slide per page), **Notes Pages** (slide + speaker notes — for the presenter), **Outline** (text only), **Handouts** (1, 2, 3, 4, 6 or 9 slides per page — for the audience).
</Reveal>

<Reveal question="6. What does 'Browsed at a kiosk' do?">
In Set Up Slide Show, it runs the presentation **full screen**, **loops continuously** and can only be stopped with **Esc** — used for unattended displays such as an open-day stand. It needs saved timings so slides advance automatically.
</Reveal>

<Takeaway>
- **F5** = from beginning, **Shift+F5** = from current slide; move with click/N/→/Space and back with P/←; **number + Enter** jumps; **B/W** blank the screen; **Esc** ends; right-click for pen, laser pointer, See All Slides.
- **Transitions** = how a slide arrives (TRANSITIONS tab: effect, Effect Options, Duration, Advance Slide On Click/After, **Apply To All**). A ★ marks slides that have one. Use **one** transition throughout.
- **Animations** = effects on objects (entrance green, emphasis yellow, exit red, motion paths); **By Paragraph** gives **progressive disclosure**. Keep them few and simple.
- **Header & Footer**: date, slide number, footer, **Don't show on title slide**, Apply to All.
- **Rehearse Timings** records how long each slide shows; **Use Timings** turns automatic advance on/off; kiosk mode loops until Esc.
- Set the **slide size** first for cards and posters. Print **Full Page Slides, Notes Pages, Outline** or **Handouts** (1–9 per page).
</Takeaway>

## Exam Tips

- Expect **"distinguish between transition and animation"** and **"list the print options"** questions.
- Remember the **keyboard** controls — they appear in short-answer questions (F5 vs Shift+F5, B, W, Esc, number + Enter).
- In a practical, markers look for: **one transition applied to all**, sensible **progressive disclosure** on bulleted slides, **slide numbers** in the footer (not on the title slide), and a **saved** file. Ten different flashy effects lose marks under the design rules.
- For a card or poster: **Slide Size → Custom** before anything else.
