## What PowerPoint Is Used For

**Microsoft PowerPoint** is widely used to make professional presentations in many formats:

- **on-screen slide shows** (projected in a lecture hall or meeting, or shared online),
- **printed** slides (colour, greyscale or black-and-white),
- **speaker's notes** for the presenter and **handouts** for the audience,
- and even as a simple **drawing package** for pictures, posters, forms, leaflets and cards.

A PowerPoint file is a **presentation**, made up of **slides**, saved as **.pptx**.

In this lesson you will build the presentation from the lecture notes — **"My Journey in UI so far"** — step by step.

## Creating the First Slide

Before typing anything, **think about your audience**: don't crowd too much information onto one slide, make sure the text is big enough to read, and keep to **about six points per slide** (the 6 × 6 rule from the previous lesson).

### Placeholders

The first slide uses the **Title Slide** layout. It contains two boxes with dotted borders called **placeholders** — reserved regions of a slide for text or graphics. The prompt inside each tells you what to do.

1. Click **Click to add title** and type the presentation title, e.g. **My Journey in UI so far**.
   - The default title font in the notes is **Calibri (Headings)** at a large **44 pt**, centre-aligned. (Office 2013's own theme uses Calibri Light.) You can change it.
2. Click **Click to add subtitle** and type **your name and matric number**.
3. If the text looks small, use the **Zoom** slider or click the zoom percentage on the status bar — zoom only magnifies the view.

Some vocabulary from the tutorial:

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Placeholder** | A region of a slide, or a location in an outline, reserved for text or graphics |
| **Text box** | An object that contains text (a placeholder that holds text is a kind of text box) |
| **Active text box** | Shows a **dashed border** and **sizing handles** while you type in it |
| **Sizing handles** | Small circles/squares on the corners and sides; drag them to resize |

### Saving

Save early and **at regular intervals** — for example every 15 minutes or after each slide — rather than at the end.

1. Click **Save** on the Quick Access Toolbar, or **FILE → Save**, or better still press **Ctrl+S**.
2. Choose the folder (in the exam: your named folder on the flash drive) and type the file name — e.g. `234567_MyJourney`.
3. PowerPoint adds the **.pptx** extension automatically. Press **Enter** for Save.

## Adding Slides and Choosing Layouts

Click the **New Slide** icon on the **HOME** tab (Slides group), or press **Ctrl+M**. The new slide appears **after the current one** and uses the **Title and Content** layout. Click the **words** "New Slide" (the arrow) instead of the icon to pick a different layout.

A **layout** is a predetermined arrangement of placeholders on a slide. Office 2013's default theme offers nine:

![Nine slide layout thumbnails with dotted placeholders: Title Slide, Title and Content, Section Header, Two Content, Comparison, Title Only, Blank, Content with Caption and Picture with Caption](/images/csc272/slide-layouts.svg)

| Layout | Use it for |
|---|---|
| **Title Slide** | The first slide: title and subtitle |
| **Title and Content** | The everyday slide: title + one content placeholder (text, table, chart, picture…) |
| **Section Header** | Dividing a long talk into parts |
| **Two Content** | Text beside a picture, or two lists side by side |
| **Comparison** | Two headed columns: before/after, advantages/disadvantages |
| **Title Only** | A title with free space for your own objects |
| **Blank** | Nothing — full-slide pictures, cards, posters |
| **Content with Caption / Picture with Caption** | An object with an explanatory caption |

On slide 2, type the title **Introduction**, click **Click to add text** and enter one point per line, pressing **Enter** after each:

- My name
- Level of study
- Course of study
- Year of entry
- State of origin

**Tip:** press **Ctrl+Enter** to jump to the next placeholder; on the last placeholder it creates a new slide.

<Analogy>
A layout is like a **form with labelled blanks**. The Title and Content form has a blank for the title and a big blank for the content. You can type on a blank sheet of paper (Blank layout), but a form keeps every slide consistent and lets PowerPoint resize pictures to fit.
</Analogy>

## Formatting the Text

The HOME tab gives you the tools. To change **all** the text in a placeholder, first **select the placeholder**: click inside it, then click on its **border** (the dashed line turns solid).

| To… | Do this |
|---|---|
| Change the **bullet character** | HOME → Paragraph → arrow next to **Bullets**; **Bullets and Numbering…** offers **Customize** (any symbol), **Picture** bullets, size and colour relative to the text |
| Use a different **font** | HOME → Font box |
| Change the **font colour** | Arrow next to **Font Colour** → a colour, or **More Colours…** |
| Change the **case** | **Change Case** → Sentence case, lowercase, UPPERCASE, Capitalize Each Word, tOGGLE cASE |
| Change **line spacing** | **Line Spacing** → 1.0, 1.5, 2.0…; **Line Spacing Options** for space before/after paragraphs |
| **Turn a bullet off** for one line | Click in that line, then click the **Bullets** icon itself (not its arrow) |
| Make a **sub-point** | Press **Tab** at the start of the line (Increase List Level); **Shift+Tab** promotes it back |

Bullets vs numbers: a **bulleted list** is a list of "paragraphs" with a special character to the left of each; a **numbered list** numbers them consecutively — use numbers when order matters.

<ExamTip>
**Changes you want on every slide** — a font, a colour, a bullet style, a logo — should be made on the **Slide Master** (VIEW → Slide Master), ideally *before* you type your slides. Formatting each slide by hand is slow and never quite consistent.
</ExamTip>

Take care with fancy bullet symbols: non-standard characters can hurt **accessibility** — stick to the offered bullets unless you know what you are doing.

## Adding a Picture

The easiest way to add a picture while keeping your text is to switch to a layout that includes a **content placeholder** for it:

1. HOME → **Layout** → **Two Content**. A new placeholder appears on the right.
2. The empty content placeholder shows six icons: **Insert Table**, **Insert Chart**, **Insert a SmartArt Graphic**, **Pictures**, **Online Pictures** and **Insert Video**.
3. Click **Pictures**, browse to the folder and **double-click** your photo.

A **content layout automatically resizes** a large picture to fit the placeholder — you don't have to shrink it by hand.

When the picture is selected, the contextual **PICTURE TOOLS → FORMAT** tab appears (it disappears when you click away):

| Task | How |
|---|---|
| **Resize** | Drag a **corner** handle (corners keep the proportions; hold **Shift** to be sure). Side handles stretch and distort it |
| **Move** | Point inside the picture and drag, or nudge with the **arrow keys** |
| **Rotate** | Drag the circular **rotation handle** at the top |
| **Exact size / rotation** | **Height** and **Width** in the Size group; **Rotate** in the Arrange group |
| **Trim edges** | **Crop** (Size group) |
| **Brightness, contrast, colour** | **Corrections** and **Color** (Adjust group) |

## Applying a Theme

A **theme** is a coordinated set of **background, colours, fonts and effects**. The default theme (black text on a white background) is plain but excellent for accessibility.

1. Click the **DESIGN** tab — the Themes gallery appears.
2. **Point** at a theme: the current slide previews it (**Live Preview**).
3. Click the **More** arrow at the bottom right of the gallery to see all themes.
4. **Click** a theme to apply it to all slides. **Right-click** a theme for **Apply to All Slides** or **Apply to Selected Slides**.
5. The **Variants** group next to Themes offers alternative colour schemes of the chosen theme.
6. Press **Ctrl+S** to save.

Normally all slides share one theme; a long presentation split into sections could use a different theme per section. Most themes pair **light text on dark** or **dark text on light** for contrast — but some contain pictures or patterns that make text hard to read. You can change the theme at any time.

**Templates** go further than themes — a template is a whole presentation file (colours, backgrounds, fonts, sometimes sample slides) that you start from: **FILE → New**, then choose or search for a template (or open a saved `.potx` template).

## PowerPoint Views

Switch views from the **VIEW** tab (Presentation Views group) or the buttons at the bottom right of the window.

| View | What you see | Use it to |
|---|---|---|
| **Normal** | Thumbnails, the slide in full, notes below | Build and edit slides; try out animation effects |
| **Outline View** | The text of all slides as an outline | Write or reorganise the text quickly |
| **Slide Sorter** | A miniature of every slide in order | Add, delete, move and hide slides; see transitions and rehearsed timings |
| **Notes Page** | One slide per page with a large notes area | Type exactly what you plan to say; this is what prints as Notes Pages |
| **Reading View** | The show inside the PowerPoint window (status bar still visible) | Review the presentation, e.g. on your own computer |
| **Slide Show** | Full screen, one slide at a time | Present, or rehearse your presentation |

### Moving between slides (Normal view)

- click the slide's **thumbnail** on the left,
- drag the **scroll bar** (the slide number shows as you drag), or click the **Previous Slide / Next Slide** double-arrows below it,
- press **Page Up / Page Down**, or the **up / down arrow** keys.

### Deleting, hiding and reordering slides

These are easiest in **Slide Sorter** view, especially with many slides.

| Task | How |
|---|---|
| **Delete** | Click the slide (thumbnail) and press **Delete**. **Ctrl+Z** (Undo) brings it back |
| **Hide** | **Right-click** the thumbnail → **Hide Slide**. The slide stays in the file but is **skipped** in the show; its number is **crossed out**. Repeat to unhide |
| **Show a hidden slide during the show** | Press **H** (when it is the next slide), or type its **number + Enter** |
| **Reorder** | Drag the slide to its new position (in Slide Sorter or the thumbnail pane). Select several with **Shift+click** (a range) or **Ctrl+click** (individual slides) |
| **Copy / move via the Clipboard** | Cut, Copy and Paste slides like any other object |
| **Duplicate** | Right-click → **Duplicate Slide** (Ctrl+D) |

Hiding is often better than deleting: keep a slide for a different audience, or a backup slide in case someone asks a particular question.

## Speaker Notes

In **Notes Page** view you can write notes to print and use while presenting — each printed page shows the slide and its notes.

1. Select a slide, then VIEW → **Notes Page**.
2. Click in the notes placeholder (**Click to add text**).
3. To read what you type, click **Zoom** on the ribbon, choose **100%**, **OK**.
4. Type your notes.
5. Return with **Normal** (status bar or VIEW tab).

You can also type notes in **Normal view** in the Notes pane under the slide (click **NOTES** on the status bar if it is hidden). Notes (also called *speaker notes*) help you remember what to say; during a show, **Presenter View** displays them on your screen only.

## Proofing and the Research Pane

- **Spelling** (REVIEW → Proofing, **F7**): checks all slides. PowerPoint also flags **contextual** errors (whose/who's).
- **Thesaurus** (REVIEW → Proofing, **Shift+F7**): a list of **synonyms**, **antonyms** and related words for the selected word — the **Research** task pane in 2013 also offers reference tools.

<Reveal question="1. What is a placeholder? Name the placeholders on the Title Slide layout.">
A **placeholder** is a region of a slide (shown with a dotted border and a prompt) reserved for inserting text or graphics. The Title Slide layout has a **title** placeholder ("Click to add title") and a **subtitle** placeholder ("Click to add subtitle").
</Reveal>

<Reveal question="2. List four views available in PowerPoint and state what each is used for.">
**Normal** — build and edit slides, with thumbnails and notes. **Slide Sorter** — see all slides as thumbnails to reorder, delete, hide and check timings. **Notes Page** — write speaker notes with a picture of the slide. **Reading View** — play the show within the window. **Slide Show** — present full screen. **Outline View** — work on the text of all slides.
</Reveal>

<Reveal question="3. How would you add a picture to an existing bulleted slide without losing the text?">
HOME → **Layout** → **Two Content**. In the new content placeholder click the **Pictures** icon, browse to the picture and double-click it. The content placeholder resizes the picture to fit; use the PICTURE TOOLS FORMAT tab to crop, resize or adjust it.
</Reveal>

<Reveal question="4. Explain the difference between applying a theme with a click and with 'Apply to Selected Slides'.">
Clicking a theme in the DESIGN tab applies it to **all** slides. Right-clicking a theme and choosing **Apply to Selected Slides** applies it only to the slides you selected — for example to give a separate section its own look.
</Reveal>

<Reveal question="5. Describe how to create an organisation chart (organogram) in PowerPoint.">
On a Title and Content slide click **Insert a SmartArt Graphic** (or INSERT → SmartArt) → **Hierarchy** → **Organization Chart** → **OK**. Type names in the shapes; right-click a shape → **Add Shape** to add boxes (after, before, above, below), press Delete to remove one.
</Reveal>

<Takeaway>
- A presentation is a set of **slides** (.pptx). New presentations start with a **Title Slide**; **New Slide** (Ctrl+M) adds a **Title and Content** slide after the current one.
- **Placeholders** are dotted boxes reserved for text or objects; a **layout** is an arrangement of placeholders (Title Slide, Title and Content, Two Content, Comparison, Blank…).
- Format all text in a placeholder by selecting its border; make deck-wide changes on the **Slide Master**.
- Two Content layout → **Pictures** icon adds a picture that auto-fits; PICTURE TOOLS FORMAT resizes, crops and rotates.
- **DESIGN → Themes** (and Variants) give a consistent professional look; right-click for Apply to Selected Slides.
- Views: **Normal, Outline, Slide Sorter, Notes Page, Reading View, Slide Show**. Slide Sorter is best for deleting, **hiding** (number crossed out) and **reordering** slides.
- Speaker notes live in the Notes pane / Notes Page view. Save often with **Ctrl+S** and spell check with **F7**.
</Takeaway>

## Exam Tips

- **Views** questions are common: list them and say what each is for. Don't confuse **Reading View** (inside the window) with **Slide Show** (full screen).
- Know the difference between **deleting** and **hiding** a slide, and the **H** key / number + Enter trick for showing a hidden slide.
- In practical tasks, **every slide needs a title**; use **layouts** instead of drawing your own text boxes everywhere, and **one theme** for the whole deck.
- An **organogram** = SmartArt → Hierarchy → Organization Chart.
- Save to the correct folder with your matric number in the file name before you start adding content.
