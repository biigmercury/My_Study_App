## Why a Spreadsheet?

Word is for **words**. When your information is mostly **numbers** that must be **calculated**, **sorted** and **charted** — scores, sales, a budget, attendance — you need a **spreadsheet**.

A **spreadsheet** is a grid of **rows and columns** whose boxes (**cells**) hold values that can be manipulated mathematically, from simple addition to complex functions. **Microsoft Excel** is the spreadsheet program in Microsoft Office. It is used for statistics, finance, data management, forecasting, inventory, billing and business intelligence, and it can:

- crunch numbers with formulas and hundreds of built-in functions,
- plot charts and graphs,
- store, import, sort and filter data,
- manipulate text,
- automate tasks with macros written in **VBA** (Visual Basic for Applications), and
- exchange data with other programs.

The course cannot cover everything Excel can do — the next five lessons build the core skills the exam tests.

## Workbook, Worksheet, Cell

<Tabs>
<Tab label="Workbook">

A **workbook** is the Excel **file** — just as a Word file is called a *document*. It is saved as **.xlsx** (Excel 97–2003: **.xls**; with macros: **.xlsm**; template: **.xltx**).

A workbook is a **collection of one or more worksheets**. You can **add**, **delete**, **rename**, **hide** (without deleting) and **reorder** worksheets. A workbook can also contain **chart sheets** — a sheet that displays a single chart, reached by clicking its tab.

</Tab>
<Tab label="Worksheet">

A **worksheet** (sheet) is one grid of cells. Each cell can contain a **value**, a **formula** or **text**. On top of the grid is an invisible **drawing layer** that holds charts, pictures and diagrams.

Each worksheet is reached by clicking its **tab** at the bottom of the window. A new **Excel 2013** workbook starts with **one** sheet, *Sheet1* (Excel 2010 and earlier started with three: Sheet1, Sheet2, Sheet3; you can change this in FILE → Options → General → *Include this many sheets*).

</Tab>
<Tab label="Cell">

A **cell** is the **smallest but most powerful** part of a spreadsheet — the box where a **column** and a **row** meet. You enter data by typing or pasting: text, a number, a date or a formula. You can format its size, font, colours and borders.

Every cell has an **address** (cell reference): its **column letter** followed by its **row number**. The cell in **column C, row 11** is **C11**.

A rectangular block of cells is a **range**, written *first cell* **:** *last cell* — **B2:D5** is the 12 cells from B2 to D5 (3 columns × 4 rows).

</Tab>
</Tabs>

<Analogy>
A workbook is an **exercise book**, each worksheet is a **page** of squared paper, and each cell is **one square** on that page. The address C11 works like a seat number in a hall: row letter and seat number together identify exactly one seat.
</Analogy>

## The Excel Window

Knowing what is where is essential before entering data. The lecture notes list twelve components:

![The Excel 2013 window with numbered labels: 1 the active cell C3 with a thick green border, 2 column B, 3 row 7, 4 the fill handle at the bottom-right of the active cell, 5 the Name Box showing C3, 6 the formula bar showing =B3*2, 7 the title bar Book1 - Excel, 8 the FILE tab, 9 the Quick Access Toolbar, 10 the Ribbon tabs HOME INSERT PAGE LAYOUT FORMULAS DATA REVIEW VIEW, 11 the worksheet tab Sheet1 and the new-sheet button, 12 the status bar showing READY and AVERAGE, COUNT and SUM](/images/csc272/excel-window.svg)

| # | Component | What it is and does |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Active cell** | The cell currently selected, outlined by a **thick border**; its address shows in the Name Box. Click a cell or use the arrow keys to make it active. **Double-click** it or press **F2** to edit its contents |
| 2 | **Column** | A **vertical** set of cells. A worksheet has **16,384** columns, lettered **A** to **XFD**. Click the **column heading** (the letter) to select the whole column |
| 3 | **Row** | A **horizontal** set of cells. A worksheet has **1,048,576** rows, numbered from **1**. Click the **row number** on the left to select the whole row |
| 4 | **Fill handle** | The small square at the **bottom-right corner** of the active cell (or selection). Drag it to fill numbers, text series (Jan, Feb…), serial numbers or formulas into neighbouring cells |
| 5 | **Name Box** ("address bar") | The small box at the left above the grid. Shows the **address of the active cell** (with a range selected, the first cell). Type an address or range here and press Enter to **jump** to it |
| 6 | **Formula bar** | The long input bar below the Ribbon. Shows the **contents** of the active cell — the formula itself, not its result — and lets you type or edit there. **✕** cancels, **✓** enters, **fx** inserts a function |
| 7 | **Title bar** | The workbook's name followed by the program name (*Book1 - Excel*) |
| 8 | **File menu (FILE tab)** | Backstage view: New, Open, Save, Save As, Print, Share, Export, Close, Options |
| 9 | **Quick Access Toolbar** | Always-visible favourite commands (Save, Undo, Redo); add your own; can be shown **below** the Ribbon |
| 10 | **Ribbon tabs** | Since Excel **2007** the old menus are replaced by the Ribbon: tabs of grouped commands |
| 11 | **Worksheet tabs** | One tab per sheet at the bottom; click to switch sheets; **⊕** adds a sheet |
| 12 | **Status bar** | The thin bar at the bottom: the **mode** (Ready, Enter, Edit, Point), instant **Average, Count and Sum** of selected numbers, view buttons (Normal, Page Layout, Page Break Preview) and the zoom slider |

A worksheet therefore has 16,384 × 1,048,576 = over **17 billion** cells — you will only ever use a tiny corner of it.

## Moving Around and Selecting

| Keys | Move to |
|---|---|
| **Arrow keys** | One cell in that direction |
| **Enter / Shift+Enter** | Down / up one cell |
| **Tab / Shift+Tab** | Right / left one cell |
| **Ctrl+Home** | Cell **A1** |
| **Ctrl+End** | The last cell that contains data |
| **Ctrl+arrow** | The edge of the current block of data |
| **Page Up / Page Down** | One screen up / down |
| **Ctrl+G** or **F5** (Go To), or type in the **Name Box** | Any address |

| To select… | Do this |
|---|---|
| A range | Drag across it, or click the first cell and **Shift+click** the last |
| Non-adjacent cells | Hold **Ctrl** while clicking or dragging |
| A column / a row | Click the **column letter** / **row number** |
| The whole worksheet | Click the **Select All** button (the corner above row 1 and left of column A), or **Ctrl+A** |

## The FILE Tab, Ribbon and Toolbars

**FILE tab (Backstage view):** as in Word, the FILE menu changed in 2013 but is still where you create a **New** file, **Open**, **Save**, **Save As**, **Close**, **Print** (with Print Preview), **Share**, **Export** and set **Options**. Click the ⮌ arrow or press **Esc** to return to the sheet.

**The Ribbon** replaced Excel's menus and toolbars from Excel 2007. It is part of the Microsoft Office **Fluent** user interface and helps you quickly find commands: **commands** are organised in logical **groups**, collected under **tabs**, each tab relating to a type of activity. Excel 2013 has **seven standard tabs**:

| Tab | Main groups | Used for |
|---|---|---|
| **HOME** | Clipboard, Font, Alignment, Number, Styles, Cells, Editing | Everyday formatting, inserting/deleting rows, AutoSum, sort and filter |
| **INSERT** | Tables, Illustrations, Charts, Sparklines, Filters, Links, Text, Symbols | Charts, pictures, PivotTables, headers & footers |
| **PAGE LAYOUT** | Themes, Page Setup, Scale to Fit, Sheet Options, Arrange | Preparing to print: margins, orientation, print area, print titles, gridlines |
| **FORMULAS** | Function Library, Defined Names, Formula Auditing, Calculation | Functions, named ranges, Show Formulas, Calculate Now |
| **DATA** | Get External Data, Connections, Sort & Filter, Data Tools, Outline | Importing, sorting, filtering, Data Validation, Remove Duplicates |
| **REVIEW** | Proofing, Language, Comments, Changes | Spelling, comments, protecting sheets and workbooks |
| **VIEW** | Workbook Views, Show, Zoom, Window, Macros | Page Layout view, gridlines, **Freeze Panes**, split, macros |

Contextual tabs appear when needed — **CHART TOOLS** (Design, Format) when a chart is selected, **TABLE TOOLS** for a formatted table, **PICTURE TOOLS** for a picture.

The **Dialog Box Launcher** (small diagonal arrow in the corner of a group such as Font, Alignment or Number) opens the full dialog box — for these three, the **Format Cells** dialog.

The **Quick Access Toolbar** holds Save, Undo and Redo by default; click the arrow at its right end to add other commands (New, Open, Quick Print, Print Preview, Spelling, Sort…) or to show it below the Ribbon.

<Reveal question="1. Define workbook, worksheet and cell.">
A **workbook** is an Excel file (.xlsx) containing one or more worksheets (and possibly chart sheets). A **worksheet** is a single grid of cells arranged in columns and rows, reached by its tab. A **cell** is the intersection of a column and a row — the smallest unit that holds a value, text or formula — identified by its address, e.g. C11.
</Reveal>

<Reveal question="2. Name and explain any six components of the Excel window.">
Any six: **active cell** (the selected cell, thick border); **column** (vertical cells, A–XFD); **row** (horizontal cells, 1–1,048,576); **fill handle** (square at the corner of the active cell, drag to fill series or copy); **Name Box** (shows the active cell's address, jump to a cell); **formula bar** (shows/edits the active cell's contents); **title bar** (workbook and program name); **FILE menu** (Backstage: New, Open, Save, Print…); **Quick Access Toolbar**; **Ribbon tabs**; **worksheet tabs**; **status bar** (mode, Sum/Average/Count, zoom).
</Reveal>

<Reveal question="3. What is the difference between the Name Box and the formula bar?">
The **Name Box** shows the **address** (or name) of the active cell and lets you jump to an address. The **formula bar** shows the **contents** of the active cell — including the formula behind a calculated value — and lets you edit them.
</Reveal>

<Reveal question="4. List the seven standard tabs of the Excel 2013 Ribbon.">
**HOME, INSERT, PAGE LAYOUT, FORMULAS, DATA, REVIEW, VIEW** (plus the FILE tab, which opens Backstage view, and contextual tabs such as CHART TOOLS).
</Reveal>

<Takeaway>
- **Excel** = spreadsheet for calculating, analysing and charting (mostly numerical) data. File = **workbook** (.xlsx) of **worksheets**; a sheet is a grid of **cells**.
- A **cell address** = column letter + row number (C11); a **range** = first:last (B2:D5).
- 16,384 columns (A–XFD) × 1,048,576 rows per sheet; Excel 2013 starts with **one** sheet.
- The 12 window parts: active cell, column, row, **fill handle**, **Name Box**, **formula bar**, title bar, FILE menu, Quick Access Toolbar, Ribbon tabs, worksheet tabs, **status bar**.
- Seven Ribbon tabs: HOME, INSERT, PAGE LAYOUT, FORMULAS, DATA, REVIEW, VIEW; Dialog Box Launchers open Format Cells and other dialogs.
</Takeaway>

## Exam Tips

- "Name and explain the components of the Excel window" is a classic — learn the **twelve** and give each a one-line function.
- Don't mix up the **Name Box** (address) and the **formula bar** (contents).
- Quote the sheet size correctly: **1,048,576 rows × 16,384 columns (A–XFD)**.
- The notes (written for Excel 2010) say a new workbook has three sheets; Excel 2013 has **one** — either answer with the version stated is accepted, but be precise.
