"With your logbook, report and evaluation form in, one stage remains for many departments: the **SIWES defence** (seminar presentation), where you present your training experience before a panel."

## What Usually Happens

- You present for **about 5 to 10 minutes**, followed by **questions**.
- The **panel is usually lecturers from your department**.
- You present **with slides**, then answer questions on **your report, logbook and actual experience**.
- Your **dressing, composure and clarity all count**.

---

## What to Present: Tell a Clear Story

<OrderPuzzle title="The seven-part story, in order">

```order
Introduction :: Who you are and where you trained.
The organisation :: What it does and the unit you worked in.
Work done :: Your main projects and tasks, with specifics.
Tools and technologies :: What you used.
Skills acquired :: Technical and soft.
Challenges :: And how you solved them.
Conclusion :: What you learned, plus any recommendations.
```

</OrderPuzzle>

### A 10-slide plan and time budget *(for an 8-minute talk)*

| Slide | Content | Time |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Title:** "SIWES Industrial Training Defence", your name, matric number, department, company | 0:20 |
| 2 | **Introduction / outline** — where, when, which unit | 0:30 |
| 3 | **The organisation** — what it does, an organogram with your unit highlighted | 1:00 |
| 4–6 | **Work done** — your **2 or 3 best projects**, one slide each, with a photo or diagram | 3:00 |
| 7 | **Tools and technologies** — logos or a short list | 0:40 |
| 8 | **Skills acquired** — two columns: technical and soft | 0:50 |
| 9 | **Challenges and solutions** — 2 or 3 problem → solution pairs | 1:00 |
| 10 | **Conclusion and recommendations** — then "Thank you — questions?" | 0:40 |

### Building your slides

- "Keep slides simple: **one idea per slide, large text, minimal decoration.**"
- **Build them straight from your report**, so the presentation and the document tell the same story.
- Aim for **roughly 8 to 12 slides**.
- If you're short on time, the guide's author built a tool called **QuickSlide** (quickslide.fun). You upload your SIWES or IT report, and it generates a structured presentation you can review and customise. The guide says over 100 Nigerian students have used it for project and SIWES presentations.

> "**However you build them, review every slide yourself.** You're the one defending, so every word on screen should be one you can stand behind."

The slide-design rules in **CSC 272** (*PowerPoint*) apply here too: contrast, font size, the 6×6 guideline, and consistent layouts.

## Common Panel Questions — Practise Them Out Loud

<QuestionDrill title="Mock SIWES panel" seconds="60">

```drill
Explain what your organisation does in one minute. :: Name, sector, main products or services, roughly how big it is, and the unit you worked in. Practise until it fits in 45–60 seconds.
What exactly was your role on this project? :: Use "I", not "we". Name the specific tasks you did, the tools, and the outcome. Be honest about what others did.
Why this tool or method instead of another? :: Give a reason: cost, the company's existing systems, ease of use, performance, or your supervisor's instruction. Mention an alternative you know of.
What was your hardest problem, and how did you solve it? :: Use the problem → cause → action → result structure, with one concrete technical detail.
How does this experience connect to your course? :: Link 2 or 3 tasks to specific courses (e.g. networking → data communications; Excel data cleaning → computer applications; PHP debugging → programming).
What would you do differently? :: Show reflection: log more detail, ask for more varied tasks earlier, learn a tool before starting. Avoid blaming the company.
On your slide you mention a LAN tester. What does it test, and what does a failed test mean? :: Anything in your report is fair game. Explain the tool simply (it checks continuity and wiring order on each pair) and what you did when a test failed.
What did you learn about working in a team? :: One specific example of communication or collaboration — a soft skill with evidence.
If the company hired you tomorrow, what could you do for them? :: Summarise your strongest practical skills confidently, with the tools named.
```

</QuestionDrill>

"**Anything in your report** is fair game. **If you wrote it, it's fair game.**"

When you don't know something, stay calm and say: *"I don't know, but here's how I'd find out."*

## How to Prepare

<Checklist title="Defence preparation (Chapter 8)">

```check
Re-read your entire report and logbook until you know them cold
Prepare clean, simple slides, roughly 8 to 12
Practise out loud at least three times, with a timer
Have a friend ask you tough questions
Dress professionally; prepare your outfit the night before
Arrive early with copies of your report and logbook
```

</Checklist>

**What to wear?** "Corporate or smart formal wear. You want the panel **focused on your presentation, not your outfit**."

## Mistakes to Avoid on the Day

<SortingGame title="Do or don't at the defence?">

```sort
@buckets Do | Don't
Reading your slides word for word with your back to the panel => Don't :: Talk to the panel; the slides are only prompts.
One idea per slide, in large text => Do :: The guide's rule for slides.
Slides crammed with paragraphs copied from your report => Don't :: Nobody can read them, and it looks unprepared.
Claiming work you didn't do => Don't :: "Panels probe, and it unravels fast."
Finishing within the time limit (5–10 minutes) => Do :: Running over time is on the guide's list of mistakes.
Getting defensive when a lecturer challenges a point => Don't :: Stay calm — "I don't know, but here's how I'd find out."
Bringing copies of your report and logbook => Do :: The panel may ask to see them.
Leaving your slides until the night before => Don't :: On the guide's list of common mistakes.
```

</SortingGame>

<Takeaway>
- **Format:** about **5–10 minutes** with slides, then **questions** from your department's lecturers. Dressing, composure and clarity count.
- **Story:** introduction → organisation → **work done** → tools → skills → challenges → conclusion.
- **Slides:** 8–12, **one idea each**, large text, built from your report. Review every slide yourself.
- **Prepare:** know your report and logbook cold, practise aloud **three times with a timer**, get tough questions from a friend, dress formally, arrive early with copies.
- **Avoid:** reading slides, text-heavy slides, **claiming work you didn't do**, running over time, getting defensive.
</Takeaway>
