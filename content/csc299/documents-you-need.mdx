## The Documents

| Document | What it does | Where it comes from |
|---|---|---|
| **Introduction Letter** | Introduces you to a company as a genuine student | Requested and downloaded on the portal |
| **Application (cover) letter** | Your formal request for a placement | **You write it** (template below) |
| **CV** (optional) | Summarises your skills and background | You prepare it |
| **Acceptance Letter** | The company's confirmation that it will train you | Issued by the company |
| **Job Reporting Form (SCAF)** | Proves you have physically started work | Portal / department; signed by your supervisor |
| **Logbook** | Records your daily activities | Issued physically; typed into the portal |
| **Technical Report** | Your full account of the training | You write it (*Writing Your SIWES Report*) |
| **Employer Evaluation Form** | Your supervisor's grading of your performance | Filled by your industry supervisor |
| **Presentation slides** | Your visual aid for the defence | You create them from your report (*Preparing for Your Defence*) |

<SortingGame title="Who produces each document?">

```sort
@buckets You | The portal / ITCC | The company / supervisor
Introduction Letter => The portal / ITCC :: Requested and downloaded on the portal.
Application (cover) letter => You :: You write it from the template.
Acceptance Letter => The company / supervisor :: Issued on the company's letterhead.
Employer Evaluation Form => The company / supervisor :: Filled by your industry supervisor, confidentially.
Technical Report => You :: Built from your logbook.
CV => You :: One page, exported as PDF.
Presentation slides => You :: Built from your report.
Signature and stamp on the Job Reporting Form => The company / supervisor :: It proves you have started work.
```

</SortingGame>

> **Keep personal copies.** "Photograph or scan **every signed document** before submitting it." Lost originals and rejected uploads are much easier to fix when you have copies.

### Make paragraph 3 specific

> 💡 **Mercury's tip.** "**Customise the third paragraph for every company.** A letter that mentions what the company actually does always beats a generic one, and it only takes five extra minutes."

Here is how the author customised it as a Physics student applying to an electrical company:

> *"…my coursework has given me a solid background in **electrical principles, circuit analysis, and instrumentation**. I'd love the chance to apply what I've learned in a practical setting, especially in areas like **electrical installations, system maintenance, or engineering design**."*

The **skills** match his course; the **interests** match the company's work.

<Reveal question="Practice: write paragraph 3 for a Computer Science student applying to (a) a bank's IT department, (b) a web-design startup, (c) a hospital's health-records unit.">
**(a) Bank IT department:** *"My studies in Computer Science have provided me with a strong foundation in **database systems, networking and programming in Java and Python**. I am keenly interested in applying this knowledge in a practical environment like yours, particularly in **IT support, network administration and the security of banking applications**."*

**(b) Web-design startup:** *"…a strong foundation in **HTML, CSS, JavaScript and user-interface design**… particularly in **building and testing responsive websites for your clients**."*

**(c) Hospital health-records unit:** *"…a strong foundation in **databases, spreadsheets and data entry and validation**… particularly in **the digitisation and management of patient records**."*

Name **real skills from your courses** (COS 201, CSC 272, COS 202…) and **one area of the company's actual work**.
</Reveal>

## Your CV at 200 Level

"You have more to put on a CV than you think":
- **education so far**, with relevant coursework;
- **personal or class projects**, however small;
- **skills**, including software tools you can use;
- **leadership roles, volunteering or club activities**.

Format: **one page, clean layout, exported as PDF.**

<DocAnatomy title="A one-page CV for a 200-level student (fictional)">

```doc
@part head = Name and contacts :: Your name, phone, professional email, city and (if you have them) LinkedIn or GitHub links. No photo, age or marital status needed.
@part profile = Profile (2–3 lines) :: Who you are and what you want from this placement — tailored to the company.
@part edu = Education :: Your degree in progress, university, years, and relevant coursework by name. Add your CGPA if it helps you.
@part proj = Projects :: Class or personal projects, however small: what you built and with which tools. This is where 200-level students stand out.
@part skills = Skills :: Programming languages, software tools (Excel, Word, PowerPoint, Access), networking basics, soft skills.
@part lead = Leadership and activities :: Class rep, club or fellowship roles, volunteering, competitions.
@part ref = References :: "Available on request" — or one lecturer who has agreed.
head^: **ADEBAYO RUTH OLUWASEUN**
head^: 0803 000 0000 · ruth.o.adebayo@example.com · Ibadan · github.com/ruthdev

profile: **PROFILE** — 200-level Computer Science student seeking a 3-month SIWES placement in software development or IT support. Quick learner, comfortable with Python, SQL and Excel.

edu: **EDUCATION**
edu: B.Sc. Computer Science (in view), University of Ibadan · 2024 – present
edu: Relevant coursework: Object-Oriented Programming (Java), Database Systems, Computer Applications (MS Office), Digital Logic

proj: **PROJECTS**
proj: • Hostel complaint tracker — a small web app (HTML, CSS, JavaScript) for logging maintenance requests
proj: • CGPA calculator in Python, used by 40+ classmates
proj: • Class results analysis in Excel: VLOOKUP, charts and conditional formatting

skills: **SKILLS** — Python · Java (basic) · SQL · HTML/CSS · MS Word, Excel, PowerPoint · Git · Teamwork and presentation

```

</DocAnatomy>

<SortingGame title="Put it on your 200-level CV?">

```sort
@buckets Yes — include it | No — leave it out
A Python CGPA calculator you built for your class => Yes — include it :: A real project, with the tool named.
Your relevant courses: Database Systems, OOP in Java => Yes — include it :: Relevant coursework, by name.
Your age, religion and marital status => No — leave it out :: Not needed, and it invites bias.

Volunteering at a tech-literacy outreach => Yes — include it :: It shows initiative and soft skills.
```

<Takeaway>
- **Nine documents:**
  - from the **portal** — the Introduction Letter;
  - from **you** — the cover letter, CV, technical report and slides;
  - from the **company** — the Acceptance Letter and the Employer Evaluation;
  - **jointly** — the SCAF (you submit, the supervisor signs and stamps) and the logbook (issued physically, typed into the portal).
- **Application letter:** use the template, and **customise paragraph 3** — your course skills plus the company's actual work.
- **CV:** education with coursework, projects, skills, leadership — **one page, PDF**.
- **Keep copies** of every signed document.
</Takeaway>
