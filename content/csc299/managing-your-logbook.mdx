## How It Works

You'll be given a **physical SIWES logbook** for your daily technical activities, and you must **also type those entries into the portal**.

<FlowDiagram title="The daily flow">
<FlowStep title="1. Do the work">
Take notes on your phone as you go: the task, the tools, the numbers.
</FlowStep>
<FlowStep title="2. Write it in the physical logbook the same day">
Neat, dated and specific. Get your supervisor's signature as required.
</FlowStep>
<FlowStep title="3. Type it into the portal regularly, entry by entry">
At least **twice a week**. Never batch it for the final week.
</FlowStep>
</FlowDiagram>

The portal's log page shows your entries **day by day, week by week** ("Week 3 of 12" in the guide's screenshot). Each entry is marked **SAVED** and can be edited or deleted. The page also has buttons to **upload your weekly scanned log** and to **chat on this week**; follow your coordinator's instruction on whether scans are required.

## What a Good Entry Looks Like

> **Weak:** *"Worked on the computer today."*
>
> **Strong:** *"Assisted in setting up the office network. Crimped RJ45 cables, tested connections with a LAN tester, and configured IP addresses on 4 workstations."*

"**Name the tools, the tasks, and what you learned.** Your logbook feeds your report and your defence, so detailed entries now mean an easier life later."

A simple pattern for every entry *(beyond the guide)*:

| Part | Question | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Task** | What did I do? | *Set up three new staff laptops* |
| **Tools** | With what? | *Windows 11 installer, Microsoft 365, the company VPN client* |
| **Result** | What came out of it, and how much? | *3 laptops ready; 1 driver problem fixed* |
| **Learning** | What did I learn or understand? | *how domain accounts and group policies are applied* |

<LogEntryCoach />

<SortingGame title="Weak or strong entry?">

```sort
@buckets Weak | Strong
Worked on the computer today. => Weak :: The guide's own example — no task, tool or outcome.
Configured a TP-Link router for the accounts unit: set the SSID and WPA2 password, reserved 5 static IPs for printers. => Strong :: Tool, task, numbers.
Did some typing in the office. => Weak :: What was typed? With what? How much?
Cleaned and validated 1,200 customer records in Excel using TRIM, Remove Duplicates and conditional formatting; found 37 duplicates. => Strong :: Specific tools and a measurable result.
Same as yesterday. => Weak :: It gives your report and your panel nothing.
Debugged the login page of the company's PHP website: a missing session_start() was causing logouts; tested the fix in Chrome and Firefox. => Strong :: Problem, cause, fix, test — great defence material.
Helped my supervisor with various tasks. => Weak :: "Various tasks" is the vaguest phrase possible.
Observed a server back-up: learnt how the weekly full back-up and daily incremental back-ups are scheduled with Windows Server Backup. => Strong :: Observation days count too — say what you learned.
```

</SortingGame>

<Reveal question="Rewrite these weak entries into strong ones: (a) 'Fixed computers.' (b) 'Worked on the website.' (c) 'Attended a meeting.'">
**(a)** *"Troubleshot 2 desktops in the accounts unit that would not boot: reseated the RAM on one, and replaced a faulty SATA cable on the other. Both now boot normally. Learnt to use POST beep codes to diagnose hardware faults."*

**(b)** *"Added a contact form to the company website using HTML and PHP, with server-side validation of email and phone fields. Tested 10 submissions, including invalid inputs."*

**(c)** *"Attended the weekly IT team meeting: the team reviewed 14 open help-desk tickets and planned the migration of staff email to Microsoft 365. I was assigned to prepare a list of affected users in Excel."*

Each answer states **what** was done, **with which tools**, **how much**, and **what was learned or decided**.
</Reveal>

### A model week (Computer Science, IT support placement)

| Day | Entry |
|---|---|
| **Mon** | Orientation by the IT supervisor: toured the server room; learnt the company's network layout (1 ISP link, 2 switches, 24 workstations). Started a note of all device names and IP ranges. |
| **Tue** | Installed Windows 11 and Microsoft 365 on 3 new staff laptops; joined them to the company domain. Learnt how group policies push settings to new machines. |
| **Wed** | Crimped 6 RJ45 cables (T568B) for the new accounts office and tested each with a LAN tester; 1 failed and was re-crimped. |
| **Thu** | Handled 5 help-desk tickets: 2 password resets, 2 printer-queue problems (cleared the print spooler), and 1 Outlook profile rebuild. |
| **Fri** | Observed the weekly server back-up; documented the restore procedure in a 2-page Word guide for the IT unit. Supervisor reviewed it and asked me to add screenshots. |

**Turned into a report paragraph later:** *"During the second week I was attached to the IT support unit, where I deployed three staff laptops, terminated and tested six network cables to the T568B standard, and resolved help-desk tickets…"* The logbook is literally your report's first draft.

## Weekly Habits

<Checklist title="Every week of training">

```check
Physical logbook filled every working day
Portal entries typed and up to date (at least twice this week)
Supervisor's signature collected as required
Photos or notes of interesting tasks saved for your report
Photographs of this week's logbook pages taken (backup)
```

</Checklist>

### Logbook FAQs (from the guide)

<Reveal question="Can I fill my logbook at the end instead of daily?">
"Some students try. **You'll forget details**, the entries will read as **generic**, and any **portal issue in the final week becomes a crisis**. Log daily, type regularly."
</Reveal>

<Reveal question="What if I lose my logbook?">
"**Report immediately to your department and the SIWES unit.** This is also why you **photograph your entries as you go**, so you can reconstruct them."
</Reveal>

<Takeaway>
- **Two records:** the **physical logbook** (written the **same day**) and the **portal training logs** (typed **at least twice a week**).
- **Strong entries** name the **task, tools, result (with numbers) and learning**. "Worked on the computer today" gives your report nothing.
- **Weekly:** logbook full, portal up to date, **signatures collected**, photos and notes saved, pages photographed.
- The logbook is your **report's first draft** and your **defence's evidence** — write it for the person who will read it later: you.
</Takeaway>
