## The Technical Report

Your **SIWES Technical Report** covers your **whole experience**: the organisation, your projects and the skills you gained.

It must be:
- **uploaded to the portal**, and
- submitted as a **hard copy** to the ITCC centre or your department.

**Both have separate deadlines**, so check your dashboard. On the guide's schedule, the portal upload window opens about three weeks before the hard-copy week.

### The nine sections

| # | Section | What goes in it |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Title page** | Your **name, matric number, department, company and training period** |
| 2 | **Acknowledgements** | Thank your industry supervisor, colleagues, SIWES coordinator and family — briefly and sincerely |
| 3 | **Introduction** | **SIWES and its objectives**: what the scheme is, why it exists, and the purpose of your attachment |
| 4 | **About the organisation** | **What the company does, its structure** (an organogram helps) **and your unit** |
| 5 | **Work done** | **The heart of the report, built from your logbook** — your projects and tasks, with specifics |
| 6 | **Skills acquired** | **Technical and soft** skills — what you can now do that you couldn't before |
| 7 | **Challenges faced** | The problems, and **how you handled them** |
| 8 | **Conclusion and recommendations** | What the experience meant for you, plus recommendations to the company, the ITCC/SIWES unit and future students |
| 9 | **References and appendices** | If needed: sources cited, photos, diagrams, sample code, forms |

## Writing Each Section Well *(beyond the guide)*

<Tabs>
<Tab label="Introduction">

Explain **SIWES**: what it is, that the **ITF** runs it, and its **objectives** (see *Understanding SIWES*). Then explain the **purpose of your own attachment**: what you hoped to learn there.

**Keep it short** (one to two pages) and **in your own words**. Panels have read the copied "SIWES was established in 1973…" paragraph hundreds of times.

</Tab>
<Tab label="The organisation">

- the company's **history, mission and services**, briefly;
- its **structure**: an **organogram** (organisational chart) with **your unit highlighted**;
- your **unit's functions**, its **equipment and facilities**, and **who supervised you**.

Use the company's brochure or website for facts, but **rewrite them in your own words and cite them**. Copying the "About us" page is **plagiarism**.

</Tab>
<Tab label="Work done">

**The heart of the report**, built from your logbook. Organise it **by project or by type of task**, not as a day-by-day diary dump. For each, cover:
- **what** the task or project was, and **why** it was needed;
- **how** you did it: steps, **tools and technologies**;
- the **outcome**, with **numbers** where possible;
- **what you learned**.

**Add evidence:** photos, labelled screenshots and diagrams — with permission, and with **no confidential data**.

</Tab>
<Tab label="Skills and challenges">

**Skills acquired**, in two lists:
- **technical** — e.g. network cabling (T568B), Windows deployment, Excel data cleaning, PHP debugging;
- **soft** — e.g. communication with non-technical staff, teamwork, time management.

**Challenges** are honest, specific problems, **each with how you handled it**: transport cost and distance, power outages, limited access to equipment, a skills gap you closed by self-study…

</Tab>
<Tab label="Conclusion">

**Conclusion:** what the attachment meant for you, and how it connects to your course.

**Recommendations**, addressed separately:
- **to the company** — e.g. a structured induction programme for SIWES students;
- **to the ITCC/SIWES unit** — e.g. earlier release of letters, supervisor visits;
- **to future students** — e.g. "log daily".

</Tab>
</Tabs>

### From logbook to "Work done"

<Reveal question="Turn these three logbook entries into one 'Work done' paragraph: (Tue) Installed Windows 11 and Microsoft 365 on 3 laptops; joined them to the domain. (Wed) Crimped 6 RJ45 cables (T568B) and tested them; 1 re-crimped. (Thu) Resolved 5 help-desk tickets: 2 password resets, 2 print-spooler issues, 1 Outlook profile rebuild.">
**4.2 IT Support and Network Maintenance**

*During my second week I was attached to the IT support unit, where I took part in three main activities.*

*First, I deployed three new staff laptops: I installed Windows 11 and Microsoft 365, then joined each machine to the company's Active Directory domain so that group policies could apply security settings automatically.*

*Second, I terminated six Ethernet cables to the T568B standard for the new accounts office and verified each with a LAN tester. One cable failed the continuity test and was re-terminated, which taught me the importance of untwisting no more than about 13 mm of each pair.*

*Third, I resolved five help-desk tickets — two password resets, two print-queue failures fixed by restarting the Print Spooler service, and an Outlook profile rebuild — which improved my troubleshooting and communication with non-technical staff.*

Notice:
- **past tense** and a **heading number**;
- the entries are **grouped by theme**, not listed by day;
- each part has **purpose, method, outcome and learning**.
</Reveal>

### Format and style

**From the guide:** "Format it properly: **consistent fonts, numbered headings, page numbers. Proofread twice.**"

**Common conventions** *(follow your department's template if it gives one)*:
- **Typography:** a plain font (e.g. Times New Roman, 12 pt), 1.5 line spacing, and about 1-inch (2.5 cm) margins.
- **Front matter:** a table of contents, a list of figures, and preliminary pages numbered i, ii, iii.
- **Chapters:** numbered (1, 1.1, 1.2), with **every figure and table numbered and captioned** — *Figure 4.1: Network layout of the accounts office*.
- **Voice:** past tense throughout. First person ("I configured…") or the passive ("The router was configured…") — be consistent.
- **Sources:** cite everything you didn't write, and keep the company's confidential data out.

Report-writing basics — structure, objectivity and recommendations — are also covered in **UIGES 201** (*Official Correspondences*).

## The Employer Evaluation Form

Towards the end of training, your supervisor fills out the **Employer Evaluation Form**, grading your **punctuality, attitude and output**. It's **confidential** — the form says it is "to be completed by the employer and returned under confidential cover through the student". Depending on the instruction given, it is **uploaded to the portal or submitted physically** to the department.

- The form pictured in the guide rates criteria such as **attendance, punctuality, comportment, willingness to take corrections, relationships with workmates, creativity, care with equipment, application of practical skills, reliability and inquisitiveness**.
- It also has space for comments on your **report and logbook**.
- **Give your supervisor the form early in the evaluation window.** Don't ask on the last day, and **never fill it yourself**.

<Takeaway>
- **Two items come due:** the **technical report** (portal upload **and** hard copy — separate deadlines) and the **Employer Evaluation Form** (confidential, filled by your supervisor).
- **Nine sections:** title page, acknowledgements, introduction, the organisation, **work done** (from the logbook), skills (technical and soft), challenges, conclusion and recommendations, references and appendices.
- **Write it in your final month**, one section at a time. Organise work done by **project**, with **tools, outcomes and learning**.
- **Format:** consistent fonts, numbered headings, page numbers, captioned figures. **Proofread twice.** No plagiarism, no confidential data.
</Takeaway>
