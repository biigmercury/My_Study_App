## The Job Reporting Form (SCAF)

Within the **first two weeks** of training, you must submit your **Job Reporting Form**, also called the **Student Commencement of Attachment Form (SCAF)**.

1. **Get the form** from the portal or your department, as directed.
2. Have your **industry supervisor sign and stamp it**. This confirms you have **physically started work**.
3. **Submit it before the deadline** on your Training Schedule.

> ⚠️ **Important.** "The SCAF is the **official proof that you commenced training**. Miss it and, as far as the records go, **you never started**. Handle it in your first week."
>
> The form itself warns: *"This form should be uploaded to the industrial training portal within the first two weeks of training. Failure to upload it is regarded as non-participation in the programme."*

## Start Strong

"Your first two weeks set the tone for the whole placement":
- **Show up early**, especially in week one.
- **Ask your supervisor what a successful placement looks like** from their side.
- **Keep a note on your phone of every task you touch.** It becomes raw material for your logbook.
- **Ask for work** instead of waiting to be found.
- **Respect everyone**, from the security officer to the manager.

> "Your supervisor grades your **punctuality, attitude and output** at the end. **That grading starts on day one**, announced or not."

### What the Employer Evaluation Form rates

The evaluation form pictured in the guide asks your supervisor to rate you on a five-point scale — **Excellent, Good, Average, Fair or Poor** — on these criteria. Each one is earned from day one:

| Criterion | How to score "Excellent" |
|---|---|
| **Attendance** | Present every working day; get permission for absences in advance |
| **Punctuality** | Arrive before resumption time, especially in week one |
| **Comportment** | Calm, polite, appropriately dressed; no drama |
| **Willingness to take corrections** | Thank people for feedback and apply it — no defensiveness |
| **Interpersonal relationship with workmates** | Greet, help, respect everyone "from the security officer to the manager" |
| **Creativity and innovation** | Suggest small improvements: a template, a script, a tidier process |
| **Carefulness in handling equipment** | Follow procedures; never touch what you weren't shown how to use |
| **Application of practical skills** | Use what you learned in class, and show it in your tasks |
| **Reliability** | Finish what you're given, on time; report problems early |
| **Inquisitiveness** | Ask good questions; take notes so you don't ask the same thing twice |

The form also asks for comments on your **written work report and logbook**, and whether the company will **take students again**. Your conduct affects the students after you.

## Workplace Conduct *(beyond the guide)*

<SortingGame title="Good move or bad move in your first weeks?">

```sort
@buckets Good move | Bad move
Arriving 15 minutes early in your first week => Good move :: Punctuality is graded from day one.
Scrolling on your phone while waiting for work => Bad move :: Ask for work instead of waiting to be found.
Writing each task in your phone notes as you finish it => Good move :: Raw material for your logbook.
Copying the company's client data to your flash drive "to study at home" => Bad move :: Company data is confidential. Never take it without permission.
Asking your supervisor, "What would a successful placement look like for you?" => Good move :: The guide recommends exactly this.
Trying a machine or server setting nobody showed you, "to learn" => Bad move :: Carefulness with equipment is graded, and you could cause damage.
Texting your supervisor before 8 a.m. when you will be late because of a traffic jam => Good move :: Communicate early and honestly.
Ignoring the cleaner and the security man because they are "not your boss" => Bad move :: Respect everyone — people talk.
Wearing appropriate, neat clothes that follow the company's dress code => Good move :: Comportment is graded.
```

</SortingGame>

<Reveal question="Situation 1: Two weeks in, your supervisor has given you nothing to do. What do you do?">
1. **Ask politely and specifically:** *"Could I shadow someone on the network team / help with the help-desk tickets / document the inventory?"*
2. **Offer something concrete**, such as organising files or writing a spreadsheet template.
3. **Keep logging** what you observe and learn.
4. If nothing changes, **inform your department's SIWES coordinator early**. A company that gives you no work is a genuine reason for a **Request for Change of Placement**, within its window.
</Reveal>

<Reveal question="Situation 2: You are asked to do something unsafe (for example, work on a live electrical panel without protective equipment).">
**Politely decline and ask for guidance or protective equipment:** *"I haven't been trained for this — could someone show me the safe procedure first?"*

Your safety comes first, and no reasonable supervisor penalises a student for following safety rules. If the pressure continues, tell your SIWES coordinator.
</Reveal>

<Reveal question="Situation 3: Your supervisor keeps postponing signing your SCAF and the deadline is in three days.">
The guide's advice:
1. **Ask politely and explain the school deadline.**
2. If it persists, **escalate respectfully within the company** — to HR, or to another manager.
3. **Inform your school-based coordinator early.**

"Don't wait until the deadline has passed."
</Reveal>

<Takeaway>
- **SCAF (Job Reporting Form):** get it, have your **industry supervisor sign and stamp it**, submit it **within the first two weeks**. Without it, "you never started".
- **Start strong:** come early, ask what success looks like, **note every task**, ask for work, respect everyone.
- **You are graded from day one** on attendance, punctuality, comportment, taking corrections, relationships, creativity, care with equipment, practical skills, reliability and inquisitiveness.
- When there is no work, an unsafe request or a delayed signature: **ask politely, escalate respectfully, tell your coordinator early**.
</Takeaway>
