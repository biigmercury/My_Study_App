# Definite Integral

The indefinite integral gave you a family of antiderivatives. The definite integral gives you a single number — the net signed area between a curve and the $x$-axis over a specific interval. This is the concept that lets calculus measure areas, volumes, accumulated change, and much more.

## Definition

Let $f$ be continuous on $[a, b]$. The **definite integral** of $f$ from $a$ to $b$ is:

$$\int_a^b f(x)\,dx$$

Here $a$ is the **lower limit** and $b$ is the **upper limit** of integration. The result is a number, not a function.

**Geometric interpretation:** If $f(x) \geq 0$ on $[a, b]$, the definite integral equals the area under the curve $y = f(x)$ above the $x$-axis between $x = a$ and $x = b$.

If $f(x)$ takes negative values, the definite integral gives the **net signed area** — area above the axis counts positive, area below counts negative.

**Real-world analogy:** Velocity $v(t)$ gives instantaneous speed at time $t$. The definite integral $\displaystyle\int_a^b v(t)\,dt$ gives the net displacement over the time interval $[a, b]$. If the object doubles back, the negative part cancels some positive area — giving displacement, not total distance.

---

## The Fundamental Theorem of Calculus

This theorem is the central result of calculus — it links the definite integral to antiderivatives.

**Fundamental Theorem (Evaluation Form):**

If $f$ is continuous on $[a, b]$ and $F$ is any antiderivative of $f$ (i.e., $F'(x) = f(x)$), then:

$$\int_a^b f(x)\,dx = F(b) - F(a)$$

**Notation:** $F(b) - F(a)$ is written as $\Big[F(x)\Big]_a^b$.

**Procedure:**
1. Find any antiderivative $F(x)$ of the integrand $f(x)$ (no $+C$ needed).
2. Evaluate $F$ at the upper limit $b$ and subtract $F$ at the lower limit $a$.

---

## Properties of the Definite Integral

Let $f$ and $g$ be integrable on $[a, b]$, and let $c$ be a constant or a point in $[a, b]$:

| Property | Statement |
|---|---|
| Reversal of limits | $\displaystyle\int_a^b f\,dx = -\int_b^a f\,dx$ |
| Zero interval | $\displaystyle\int_a^a f\,dx = 0$ |
| Constant multiple | $\displaystyle\int_a^b cf\,dx = c\int_a^b f\,dx$ |
| Sum/Difference | $\displaystyle\int_a^b [f \pm g]\,dx = \int_a^b f\,dx \pm \int_a^b g\,dx$ |
| Additivity | $\displaystyle\int_a^b f\,dx = \int_a^c f\,dx + \int_c^b f\,dx$ |
| Non-negativity | If $f(x) \geq 0$ on $[a,b]$, then $\displaystyle\int_a^b f\,dx \geq 0$ |
| Comparison | If $f(x) \geq g(x)$ on $[a,b]$, then $\displaystyle\int_a^b f\,dx \geq \int_a^b g\,dx$ |

## Example 1 — Direct Evaluation

**Problem:** Evaluate $\displaystyle\int_1^3 (2x^2 - 3x + 1)\,dx$.

**Step 1:** Find the antiderivative.
$$F(x) = \frac{2x^3}{3} - \frac{3x^2}{2} + x$$

**Step 2:** Apply the Fundamental Theorem.
$$\int_1^3 (2x^2 - 3x + 1)\,dx = \left[\frac{2x^3}{3} - \frac{3x^2}{2} + x\right]_1^3$$

At $x = 3$: $\dfrac{2(27)}{3} - \dfrac{3(9)}{2} + 3 = 18 - \dfrac{27}{2} + 3 = 21 - 13.5 = 7.5$

At $x = 1$: $\dfrac{2}{3} - \dfrac{3}{2} + 1 = \dfrac{4 - 9 + 6}{6} = \dfrac{1}{6}$

$$\int_1^3 (2x^2 - 3x + 1)\,dx = 7.5 - \frac{1}{6} = \frac{45}{6} - \frac{1}{6} = \frac{44}{6} = \frac{22}{3}$$

## Example 2 — Trigonometric Integral

**Problem:** Evaluate $\displaystyle\int_0^{\pi/2} \cos x\,dx$.

$$\int_0^{\pi/2} \cos x\,dx = \Big[\sin x\Big]_0^{\pi/2} = \sin\frac{\pi}{2} - \sin 0 = 1 - 0 = 1$$

## Example 3 — Using Properties

**Problem:** Given $\displaystyle\int_{-10}^{6} f(x)\,dx = 23$ and $\displaystyle\int_{-10}^{6} g(x)\,dx = -9$, evaluate:

$$\int_6^{-10} [2f(x) - 10g(x)]\,dx$$

**Step 1:** Reverse the limits.
$$\int_6^{-10} [2f - 10g]\,dx = -\int_{-10}^6 [2f - 10g]\,dx$$

**Step 2:** Split by linearity.
$$= -\left[2\int_{-10}^6 f\,dx - 10\int_{-10}^6 g\,dx\right]$$

**Step 3:** Substitute known values.
$$= -[2(23) - 10(-9)] = -[46 + 90] = -136$$

## Example 4 — Substitution in Definite Integrals

**Problem:** Evaluate $\displaystyle\int_0^1 2x(x^2 + 1)^3\,dx$.

**Method:** Use substitution $u = x^2 + 1$, so $du = 2x\,dx$.

**Change limits:** When $x = 0$: $u = 1$. When $x = 1$: $u = 2$.

$$\int_0^1 2x(x^2 + 1)^3\,dx = \int_1^2 u^3\,du = \left[\frac{u^4}{4}\right]_1^2 = \frac{16}{4} - \frac{1}{4} = 4 - \frac{1}{4} = \frac{15}{4}$$

---

## Example 5 — Absolute Value Integrand

**Problem:** Evaluate $\displaystyle\int_{-2}^{3} |x|\,dx$.

Split at $x = 0$ (where the sign of $x$ changes):

$$\int_{-2}^3 |x|\,dx = \int_{-2}^0 (-x)\,dx + \int_0^3 x\,dx$$

$$= \left[-\frac{x^2}{2}\right]_{-2}^0 + \left[\frac{x^2}{2}\right]_0^3$$

$$= \left(0 - \left(-\frac{4}{2}\right)\right) + \left(\frac{9}{2} - 0\right) = 2 + \frac{9}{2} = \frac{13}{2}$$

## Average Value of a Function

The **average value** of a continuous function $f$ on $[a, b]$ is:

$$f_{\text{avg}} = \frac{1}{b - a} \int_a^b f(x)\,dx$$

**Interpretation:** This is the height of the rectangle with base $[a, b]$ that has the same area as the area under the curve.

---

## Example 6 — Average Value

**Problem:** Find the average value of $f(x) = \sin x$ on $[0, \pi]$.

$$f_{\text{avg}} = \frac{1}{\pi - 0}\int_0^\pi \sin x\,dx = \frac{1}{\pi}\Big[-\cos x\Big]_0^\pi = \frac{1}{\pi}(-\cos\pi + \cos 0) = \frac{1}{\pi}(1 + 1) = \frac{2}{\pi}$$

---

## Key Takeaways

- The definite integral $\displaystyle\int_a^b f(x)\,dx$ gives the net signed area between $f$ and the $x$-axis on $[a, b]$.
- The Fundamental Theorem: $\displaystyle\int_a^b f\,dx = F(b) - F(a)$ where $F' = f$ — evaluate the antiderivative at the limits and subtract.
- Reversing limits negates the integral: $\displaystyle\int_a^b = -\int_b^a$.
- For substitution in definite integrals: change the limits of integration when you change the variable.
- Average value: $\dfrac{1}{b-a}\displaystyle\int_a^b f\,dx$.

---

## Exam Tips

- Always simplify the antiderivative before substituting limits — arithmetic errors at the evaluation step are common.
- When using the Fundamental Theorem, there is no $+C$ needed — constants cancel in $F(b) - F(a)$.
- For "given $\int f = ...$, find $\int [af + bg]$" problems: use linearity and reversal-of-limits properties step by step, showing every substitution.
- Absolute value integrands: always split at the points where the expression inside changes sign.
- At UI, definite integral evaluation and properties questions frequently appear. Know all seven properties in the table above.
